What changed

The opportunity is bigger than the plan we showed you.

Our first round funded a product for our own events. This round funds the company that runs everyone else's. Three things moved us:

First mover

No one has built this yet

The software that ran festivals is gone, and nothing built for agents has replaced it. Moving slowly gives others time to copy us.

The hard part

Intelligence, not code

Software is getting cheaper to build. Training systems that forecast event risk and money is not, and errors there are expensive. That takes data science talent.

Unfair advantage

We run the test events

Four IMXP events in 2027 give Orion real users, real money and hard deadlines no competitor can buy.

Structure

Investors own the product company. The events pay to use it.

Orion lives in IMXP Product, Inc., a Delaware C-corp. Investors buy into that company only, separate from event risk.

Parent

Immersive Experiences, LLC

Produces the events and holds event revenue and risk. Owns 80% of IMXP Product, Inc. after this round.

License + transaction feesOrion platform
Where you invest

IMXP Product, Inc.

Owns Orion: code, models, data and IP, formally assigned from the LLC before close. Earns revenue from every event that runs on it.

  • Intercompany license. The LLC pays the C-corp a per-event license plus a fee on the transactions Orion handles, on arm's-length terms set with our accountant.
  • Clean IP. All Orion code, data and trademarks are assigned to the C-corp before funds are wired.
  • Shared people, priced fairly. Team members who work across both companies are allocated by time, documented in a services agreement.

Outcome

Built to be the system the biggest event companies need.

Live entertainment's leaders spend almost every dollar they earn on costs. Orion cuts that cost and keeps what each event learns. That makes it valuable to the companies that run the most events.

Strategic buyers

Promoters, ticketing and experience platforms

Companies such as Live Nation, Fever and Eventbrite already own the audiences and the money flows. Orion runs the production between them.

Why keep it in-house now

The data is the moat

Running Orion on our own events first builds the decision data competitors can't copy. That raises the price of a strategic exit more than early licensing revenue would.
